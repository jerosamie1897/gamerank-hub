BUILD BETTER HABITS

Beginner gaming guide: settings, aim, movement and strategy

Better settings help, but consistent improvement comes from simple habits: noticing mistakes, practicing one skill at a time, and making good decisions under pressure.

Reviewed August 8, 2026 · 7 minute read

By the GameRank Hub Editorial Team

The fastest way to get better at games

Focus on one repeatable skill instead of trying to fix everything in a single session. A useful goal is specific and observable: checking the map every ten seconds, keeping your crosshair at the expected target height, or saving one resource for the next objective.

A simple improvement loop

Choose one skill, practice it for several matches, review one mistake, and set a smaller goal for the next session.

START WITH STABILITY

Game settings that matter most

DISPLAY

Prioritize a stable frame rate

Consistent performance makes timing easier to learn. Lower demanding visual effects before reducing interface clarity or useful draw distance.

CONTROLS

Use a repeatable sensitivity

Avoid changing sensitivity after every difficult match. Give your hands time to build familiarity before making small adjustments.

AUDIO

Balance information and comfort

Keep important cues audible without setting the overall volume so high that longer sessions become uncomfortable.

ACCESSIBILITY

Use tools that improve clarity

Subtitles, color filters, hold/toggle options, remapping, and camera controls can make play clearer and more comfortable.

LEARN THE BASICS

Core skills that transfer between games

Mechanics vary, but several skills transfer well: camera control, spacing, timing, resource management, and recognizing safe versus risky positions. Learn what information the interface provides and connect each cue to a decision.

MAKE BETTER DECISIONS

Strategy for new players

Good strategy begins with understanding the objective and the tradeoffs around it. Before taking a risk, ask what you gain, what you could lose, and whether your team can act with you. Reliable simple plans usually outperform clever plans that require perfect execution.

Do not copy an expert move without understanding the information that made the move correct.

COMMUNICATE CLEARLY

How to be a better teammate

  • Share useful information: Say what happened, where it happened, and what you can do next.
  • Keep calls short: Important information is easier to use when it is calm and specific.
  • Match the group's plan: A coordinated good plan is often stronger than an isolated perfect play.
  • Reset after mistakes: Discuss detailed feedback after the match rather than during the next critical moment.
